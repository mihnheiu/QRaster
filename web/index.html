📖 User Guide & QR Technical Knowledge

📌 What is a QR Code?

A QR Code (Quick Response Code) is a 2D matrix barcode invented by Denso Wave in 1994 and standardized under ISO/IEC 18004.

Unlike standard 1D barcodes that hold small numbers, QR codes can store thousands of characters, URLs, or binary data with built-in fault tolerance via Galois Field Reed-Solomon error correction.

📐 Detailed QR Code Structure

  • Finder Patterns (7x7): 3 square patterns located at Top-Left, Top-Right, and Bottom-Left corners to establish orientation and scale.
  • Timing Patterns: Alternating black and white modules along Row 7 and Column 7 connecting the Finder Patterns to determine module coordinates.
  • Alignment Patterns: Positioned across Version 2 ($25 \times 25$) to Version 40 ($161 \times 161$) matrices to prevent warping on curved surfaces.
  • Format Info & 8 Mask Patterns: Contains error correction level (L, M, Q, H) and 1 of 8 XOR masking patterns to break up large uniform areas.
  • Reed-Solomon Error Correction (Galois Field GF(256)): Mathematical engine that restores missing/damaged payload bytes automatically.

📊 How the Readability Meter Works

The Readability Meter evaluates real-time QR integrity based on ISO 18004 standards:

  • 0% (Empty Grid): When no dark modules exist, Readability is strictly 0%.
  • Max 30% (Structural Patterns): Drawing all 3 Finder Patterns and Timing Patterns yields up to 30% structural readiness.
  • Remaining 70% (Data Payload & Reed-Solomon): As you draw/edit 70-80% of data modules, the GF(256) Reed-Solomon engine automatically calculates missing bytes and **decodes the string in real-time**!
